# Metric definitions and assumptions

> **Everything in RetailIQ is SIMULATED.** The business, customers, reviews and numbers are generated by a seeded program. Nothing here describes a real company, and no result should be quoted as a real-world finding.

## 1. Conventions used everywhere

| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| Currency | All amounts are stored in **INR** and exclude GST. Other display currencies are a *display conversion* at a rate you type in the sidebar (the pre-filled rates are illustrative placeholders, not market rates). Percentages and ratios never change with currency. |
| Reference date | The "as at" date for balances. Defaults to the **latest date in the data** and can be changed in the sidebar. |
| Period vs balance | **Period measures** (sales, profit, spend, cash movements) are summed over a date range. **Balances** (cash, inventory, receivables, payables, loans) are measured at one date. The two are never mixed in one number. |
| Comparisons | "Previous period" = the equal-length window ending the day before the period starts. "Same period last year" is shown only when the data goes back far enough. A comparison with a zero or missing base shows nothing rather than a misleading percentage. |
| Revenue recognition | Revenue is booked on the sale date. Refunds are booked on the return date. |
| Expenses | Recognised **and paid on the same date** (no accruals are simulated). Rent is paid on the 1st, wages at month end, and so on. Because of this, a period that is not a set of whole months can show lumpy profit; charts therefore use whole months only. |
| Tax | No income tax is modelled. The bottom line is "profit before tax". |
| Zero or missing data | Ratios with a zero denominator return *n/a* (never infinity or a silent zero). Empty filters show a message instead of an empty chart. |

## 2. Profit and loss (module `analytics/finance.py`)

| Line | Definition |
|---|---|
| Gross sales | Quantity × list price of every line sold |
| Discounts | Promotion, loyalty and trade discounts |
| Returns (refunds) | Refunds paid in the period (full-line returns) |
| **Net sales** | Gross sales − discounts − returns |
| **COGS** | Weighted-average cost of units sold − cost of units returned to stock. **Buying stock is not an expense**: purchases raise inventory; they reach COGS only when the goods are sold. |
| **Gross profit** | Net sales − COGS. *Not net profit.* |
| Operating expenses | Rent, wages, utilities, repairs, software, insurance, office costs, card fees, delivery, packaging, marketing. **Capital expenditure is excluded.** |
| Depreciation | Straight line on capital expenditure, starting the month *after* purchase, one charge on each month end. Derived, not stored. |
| **Operating profit** | Gross profit − operating expenses − depreciation |
| Interest expense | Interest portion of loan instalments. **Loan principal is never an expense.** |
| **Profit before tax** | Operating profit − interest expense |
| Gross margin / operating margin | Gross profit ÷ net sales / operating profit ÷ net sales |

**Financing is not income.** Loans drawn and owner investment are *financing cash inflows*; repayments of principal and owner drawings are *financing outflows*. None of them touch net sales or operating profit.

### Allocation of shared costs

Head-office costs (head-office payroll, software, insurance, office costs, and any marketing campaign that is not tied to one store), depreciation of shared assets and loan interest have no store. When you look at one store (or a subset), they are allocated by **share of net sales in the period** (default) or by an **equal split** (your choice on the Finance page). The whole company always carries 100%. The Finance page shows that store profits add up to the company profit. Product-level "contribution" allocates only *variable* selling costs (card fees, delivery, packaging) by net sales within each store; fixed costs are deliberately **not** pushed down to products.

### Contribution margin and break-even

- Variable costs = COGS + card fees + delivery + packaging. Fixed costs = all other operating expenses (marketing is treated as a period cost) + depreciation.
- Contribution margin ratio = (net sales − variable costs) ÷ net sales.
- Break-even sales = fixed costs ÷ contribution-margin ratio, at the period's own mix and prices. Margin of safety = (sales − break-even) ÷ sales. If the ratio is zero or negative, or there are no sales, the page says so instead of dividing.

### Budget vs actual

The `budgets` table holds a monthly plan per store (and `HQ` for shared costs) for net sales, COGS, operating expenses and depreciation. Variance sign: **positive = favourable to profit** (sales above budget, costs below budget). Head-office costs are budgeted but not allocated to stores.

## 3. Cash and the balance sheet

- **Cash ledger** (`cash_ledger`) holds one row per cash movement, labelled *operating*, *investing* or *financing*: customer collections, refunds, supplier payments, operating expenses (operating); capital expenditure (investing); loan drawdowns, loan interest, loan principal, owner investment, owner drawings (financing).
- **Reconciliation**: opening cash + inflows − outflows = closing cash. The app also rebuilds cash directly from the source tables (receipts, refunds, supplier payments, expenses, capex, loans, equity) and shows the two agree.
- **Opening balances** are documented in the `opening_balances` table: cash ₹9.0 lakh (an assumption), receivables (business invoices outstanding), inventory (opening stock at initial cost), net fixed assets (pre-period capex less depreciation), payables (opening supplier invoices), loan principal outstanding, and **owner equity as the balancing figure**.
- **Balance sheet identity**: assets (cash + receivables + inventory + net fixed assets) = liabilities (payables + loans) + equity (opening equity + owner investment − drawings + profit before tax since the opening date). The app checks this holds to the paisa on any date.

## 4. Working capital (module `analytics/capital.py`)

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| Operating working capital (the "working-capital requirement") | Inventory + receivables − payables |
| Inventory days | Inventory at the date ÷ (COGS per day over the window) |
| Receivable days | Receivables at the date ÷ (net sales per day over the window) |
| Payable days | Payables at the date ÷ (COGS per day over the window) |
| **Cash conversion cycle** | Inventory days + receivable days − payable days |
| Estimated minimum operating cash | Average daily operating cash outflow over the window × cover days (default 14) |

Window = trailing 90 days (editable). If fewer than 28 days of history exist the page warns that the days metrics are unreliable; if sales or COGS in the window are zero the days metrics are *n/a*. Receivables include card/UPI settlements in transit (1–2 days) and business customers on 30-day credit; receivable days are therefore low.

**Scenario tool.** Inputs: sales growth, gross margin, inventory days, collection days, payable days, minimum cash reserve. Daily sales are scaled by (1 + growth); daily COGS = scenario sales × (1 − margin); inventory = COGS/day × inventory days; receivables = sales/day × collection days; payables = COGS/day × payable days. The result is the *extra cash tied up* (scenario requirement − today's), the cash left after funding it, and any shortfall against the reserve you chose. A sensitivity table changes one assumption at a time. The model captures only the **timing of cash in working capital** — not extra profit, tax, or new equipment.

## 5. Inventory and procurement (module `analytics/inventory.py`)

- **Valuation**: weighted-average cost per store × product. A receipt re-averages the unit cost; a sale leaves at the current average; a return re-enters at the cost it left with. Stock movements always reconcile with balances.
- **Daily demand** (d): *recent average* of net units (sold − returned) over the last N days (default 90), or *seasonal* = the same dates one year earlier × the recent year-on-year growth (capped 0.5×–2×), when at least ~13 months exist.
- **Lead time** (LT): the supplier's *observed* mean order-to-receipt days over the last 12 months, and its standard deviation.
- **Safety stock** = z × √(LT × σd² + d² × σLT²); z = 1.65 for a 95% service level by default.
- **Reorder point** = d × LT + safety stock. **Order-up-to level** = d × (LT + cover days) + safety stock (cover default 30 days).
- **Suggested quantity** = max(0, order-up-to − (on hand + on order)), rounded **up** to the supplier case pack and capped at the supplier's maximum order size; flagged when capped. Never negative; stock on order is always subtracted.
- **Days of stock** = on hand ÷ d. **Turnover** = annualised COGS ÷ average inventory value. **GMROI** = annualised gross profit ÷ average inventory at cost.
- **Status**: *Out of stock* (nothing on hand), *Low stock – reorder* (on hand + on order ≤ reorder point), *Overstock* (cover including on-order above your limit, default 120 days). **Movement**: *Fast* (days of stock ≤ 30), *Slow* (> 120), *No sales*.
- **Budget prioritisation** (when money is limited): Phase 1 funds the *essential* units (back to the reorder point), ranked by *profit at risk per rupee* = d × unit margin × days of lead time not covered by stock ÷ essential cost. Phase 2 funds extra cover, ranked by GMROI. Units are bought in whole case packs and the last line may be partly funded. It is a transparent rule of thumb, not an optimiser; trade-offs are explained on the page.

## 6. 13-week cash forecast and funding (modules `analytics/cashflow.py`, `funding.py`)

The forecast is built day by day and shown by week. Every component is documented on the page; in summary:

| Line | Method |
|---|---|
| Sales | Same weekday one year earlier × each store's recent year-on-year growth × (1 + your adjustment). Festival dates that move between years (e.g. Diwali) are **not** shifted. |
| Collections | Cash/UPI/card settle in 0/1/2 days. Business-credit sales follow the last 12 months' collection pattern. Invoices already outstanding are collected on their due date plus typical lateness, never before tomorrow. |
| Supplier payments | Unpaid invoices on their due date. Open purchase orders arrive at the expected date plus typical lateness and are paid after the supplier's terms. **New** purchase orders come from a day-by-day replenishment simulation using the reorder rules above (cover default 40 days in the forecast). |
| Operating expenses | Fixed items repeat last month's amounts on the same day of the month. Variable costs follow their recent ratio to sales. Marketing follows planned campaign budgets (paid at month end). |
| Loans | Contractual instalments; **interest and principal are separate lines**. |
| Capex, drawings, other | The editable "known future cash items" table (starts from the database's `planned_cash_items`). Edits live in the browser session only. |

The shortfall check uses the **daily** closing balance against your minimum reserve and reports the first day below it, the lowest point and the size of the gap. Not modelled: income tax, GST timing, price changes, one-off events, stock-outs reducing sales.

**Funding comparison** (all rates, fees, dates and amounts are *your* inputs — nothing is a real bank offer):

- **Owner capital**: cash in on the date; no interest or repayment. Optional simple dilution: investor share = investment ÷ (pre-money + investment).
- **Term loan**: principal received less an upfront fee on the drawdown date; equal monthly instalments (optional interest-only grace months). Monthly interest = opening balance × annual rate ÷ 12; a 0% loan simply repays principal. The effective annual cost is the annualised IRR of the net proceeds and instalments, including fees.
- **Revolving credit facility**: simulated daily — draw just enough to keep cash at the reserve (up to the limit), repay from surplus cash; interest on the drawn balance and a commitment fee on the undrawn part, paid at each month end.
- **Operational changes**: buy less stock, pay suppliers later, collect credit invoices sooner (these re-run the forecast).

The comparison shows consequences under assumptions. It does **not** pick a "best" option: risk, security demanded by lenders, control and tax are not modelled.

## 7. Marketing (module `analytics/marketing.py`)

**Attribution (simulated).** Each sale carries at most **one** campaign: the one whose code the shopper redeemed. This is single-touch, redemption-based attribution. Attributed revenue therefore can never exceed company revenue and is never double counted; the app proves this on the Marketing page. It is **not** evidence that advertising caused the sale.

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| CTR | clicks ÷ impressions |
| Cost per click | spend ÷ clicks |
| Conversion rate | attributed orders ÷ **clicks** (the denominator is clicks; it can exceed 100% for offline channels such as print/QR) |
| Cost per attributed order (CPA) | spend ÷ attributed orders |
| Cost per new customer (CAC) | spend ÷ customers whose first-ever purchase was attributed to the campaign |
| Attributed revenue | Net amount (after discounts, before returns) of attributed sales |
| **ROAS** | attributed revenue ÷ spend — *a revenue ratio, not profit* |
| **Contribution** | attributed revenue − refunds − COGS (net of returns) − estimated variable selling costs − media spend |
| Adjusted contribution | Contribution with an assumed share *q* of sales removed as "would have happened anyway": (1 − q) × contribution-before-media − q × discounts given − spend |

Variable selling costs use the company's actual ratio of card fees + delivery + packaging to sales in the period. The **budget scenario** applies a diminishing-returns assumption: new revenue = revenue × (new spend ÷ spend)^elasticity (default 0.6) — an input, not a measured relationship.

## 8. Customers (module `analytics/customers.py`)

Only **identified** customers (loyalty and business accounts) can be tracked; anonymous walk-in sales are excluded and their revenue share is displayed.

- **New customer**: first purchase observed in the data *and* registered on/after the dataset start. Earlier-registered customers are "returning" (their history is not in the data).
- **Repeat-purchase rate**: customers with ≥ 2 orders ÷ customers with ≥ 1 order, in the trailing 12 months.
- **Purchase frequency** = orders per active customer; **AOV** = net sales ÷ orders.
- **RFM**: recency (days since last purchase), frequency (orders, 12 months), monetary (net spend after returns, 12 months), each scored 1–5 by rank among active customers. Segments: Champions (R≥4, F≥4), Loyal (R≥3, F≥3), New or promising (R≥4, F≤2), At risk (R≤2, F≥4), Hibernating (R≤2), otherwise Needs attention; no purchase in the window = Lapsed.
- **Inactivity rule**: *Active* < 60 days since last purchase; *Becoming inactive* 60–119; *Inactive* ≥ 120 (editable).
- **Cohort retention**: share of each first-purchase-month cohort that bought again *k* months later; months not yet observable are blank.
- **Lifetime value**: CLV = (orders per year × AOV × gross margin) × expected lifetime, expected lifetime = 1 ÷ (1 − annual retention) capped at 5 years. Observation: the latest 12 months for activity and the 12 months before for retention. **Limits**: constant retention and margin, undiscounted, ignores acquisition cost, needs 24 months of data, and the data is simulated.

## 9. Customer reviews (module `analytics/reviews.py`)

- **Sentiment baseline**: counts words from two short lists (with simple negation); score = (positive − negative) ÷ total hits; > +0.2 positive, < −0.2 negative, otherwise neutral. It misses sarcasm, context and unlisted words. Every score can be inspected on the page.
- **Agreement with stars** is reported but is **not** accuracy: the review text is generated from templates, so the words line up with the ratings far better than real reviews would.
- **Themes**: price, quality, delivery, availability, service — detected by keyword lists (in code); a review can have several.
- **Falling ratings**: average rating in the last 90 days is ≥ 0.4 below the previous 180 days (≥ 8 reviews in each).
- **Repeated complaints**: ≥ 5 negative (≤ 2 stars or negative text) reviews on one theme in the last 120 days making up ≥ 25% of the product's reviews.

## 10. Sales forecasting (module `analytics/forecasting.py`)

- Target: **units per day** for every store × product, 7- and 30-day horizons.
- **Baseline**: seasonal naive — each future day repeats the same weekday of the last observed week.
- **Model**: Ridge regression (alpha 5, standardised inputs) on lags 1/7/14/28, 7- and 28-day rolling means of the *previous* days, weekday, month (sine/cosine) and a planned-promotion flag from the campaign calendar.
- **Leakage prevention**: features for day *t* use only days before *t*; multi-day forecasts are recursive (day 2 uses the forecast for day 1, never the actual); only `y[:origin+1]` is read; the promotion flag comes from the plan, not from sales; validation is chronological.
- **Validation**: six rolling origins, 28 days apart; train strictly before the origin, test strictly after. **MAE** (units per day) and **WAPE** (Σ|error| ÷ Σ actual — defined even when some days have zero sales) are reported for both methods, plus bias and the error of the horizon *total*.
- **Safe handling**: fewer than 84 days of history → baseline; no sales in the last 28 days → zero forecast; forecasts are never negative.
- Reported scores describe how the method behaves **on this simulated dataset**, not real-world accuracy.

## 11. Action Center rules (module `analytics/actions.py`)

Default thresholds (all editable on the page). Each finding shows the rule, the numbers, the reference period, a suggested action and the limitations.

| Id | Rule (fires when…) |
|---|---|
| R01 | Lowest projected daily cash in the next 13 weeks is below the minimum reserve (default ₹750,000) |
| R02 | The same, under a stress test (sales −20%, credit customers 15 days later) |
| R03 | A product with margin ≥ 25% and recent sales is out of stock, or stock covers fewer days than the lead time and the position is below the reorder point |
| R04 | Slow-moving / no-sales stock is worth ≥ ₹50,000 |
| R05 | Net sales over the latest three whole months are higher than the same months a year earlier (or the previous three) but operating profit is lower |
| R06 | A campaign has ROAS ≥ 3.0 but contribution per rupee spent < 0.10 |
| R07 | A product has ≥ 5 negative quality-theme reviews in 120 days making up ≥ 25% of its reviews |
| R08 | A product's rating fell ≥ 0.4 stars (recent 90 days vs prior 180) |
| R09 | A supplier's on-time share is below 80% (≥ 10 orders in 12 months) |
| R10 | Overdue business invoices total ≥ ₹50,000 |
| R11 | ≥ 10 repeat customers are 60–119 days since their last purchase |
| R12 | Operating expenses for the latest three months exceed budget by more than 10% |
| R13 | A reconciliation check fails (balance sheet, cash, stock) beyond ₹1 |

Rules that do not fire are listed under "Checked and not triggered", so you can see what was examined. No external AI service is used; the same data always gives the same findings.

## 12. How the simulation is built (assumptions you should know)

The generator (`retailiq/datagen/`) runs a **day-by-day simulation** with a fixed random seed (default 42), so the same command always makes the same data.

- **Business**: two stores (Bengaluru "Central", opened 2021; Pune "Lakeside", opened 2023), 20 products in 6 categories, 6 suppliers with quoted lead times (4–14 days), reliability (72–97% on time) and payment terms (15–45 days), about 1,240 customers (1,200 loyalty customers plus 40 business accounts with 30-day credit).
- **Demand**: store traffic × month seasonality × weekday pattern × a Diwali bump × category/product seasonality (e.g. winter hoodies, back-to-school stationery, new-year fitness) × campaign uplifts; baskets of 1–4 lines; business customers place occasional bulk orders.
- **Stock**: sales leave stock at weighted-average cost; if stock is short the customer buys less. Each evening each product is checked against a reorder point; purchase orders are grouped per supplier; some deliveries arrive late.
- **Money**: opening cash ₹9.0 lakh; supplier unit costs were scaled so the gross margin lands near 40% and the operating margin is thin; two term loans, one owner investment and quarterly owner drawings; six capital purchases; monthly rent, wages, utilities and other costs; card fees (1.8%), packaging (0.6% of sales) and delivery (₹70 per online order).
- **Patterns deliberately built in** (so the Action Center and analytics have something real to find — they are *not* discoveries): supplier price rises (+6% from Oct 2025 for the electronics supplier, +5% from Apr 2026 for the apparel supplier); wage and rent step-ups; a fading product (Bluetooth speaker) with clearance discounts from Jun 2026; a defect in the steel water bottle from Mar 2026 (higher returns, bad reviews); a service problem at the Pune store May–Aug 2026; two deep-discount electronics campaigns with strong ROAS but weak profit; unreliable deliveries from two suppliers; late-paying business customers.
- **Reviews**: generated from sentence templates, with ratings influenced by product quality, late delivery, stock-outs, service problems and discounts.
- **Marketing**: media spend is budget ÷ days with noise; impressions follow a cost-per-thousand, clicks a channel click-through rate; attribution is by code redemption (see §7).
- **Not simulated**: tax, GST, accruals, supplier price negotiation, staff turnover, theft or wastage, inflation beyond the listed price steps, multiple currencies.
